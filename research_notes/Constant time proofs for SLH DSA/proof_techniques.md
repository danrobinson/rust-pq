## Key Question 1: What are the standard leakage models and how do they define "trace"? (ct-verif, Barthe et al. CCS 2014, "Verifying constant-time implementations")

### Takeaway
All mainstream work defines constant-time as observational non-interference over a leakage-instrumented semantics: fix a function from execution steps to observations, and require that any two runs from states agreeing on public data produce equal observation sequences. The models differ only in what a step emits: branch outcomes (program-counter model), plus accessed addresses or array indices (the "constant-time" or "cryptographic constant-time" model), plus operands or operand sizes of variable-time instructions (the "size-respecting"/DOIT-style models).

### Cited Findings

Naming
- "Verifying Constant-Time Implementations" is the ct-verif paper itself (Almeida, Barbosa, Barthe, Dupressoir, Emmi; USENIX Security 2016), not a separate work. — [ct-verif paper](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity16/sec16_paper_almeida.pdf)

ct-verif formal definitions (2016)
- Language: while programs with arrays and assert/assume. A configuration is `c = ⟨s, p⟩`; an execution is a sequence `c1…cn`. "A leakage model L maps program configurations c to observations L(c), and extends to executions, mapping c1c2…cn to the observation L(c1c2…cn) = L(c1)·L(c2)···L(cn)". Two executions are indistinguishable when their observations are equal. — [ct-verif paper, §3](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity16/sec16_paper_almeida.pdf)
- Path-based (program-counter) model: `⟨s, if e then p1 else p2⟩ ↦ s(e)` and `⟨s, while e do p⟩ ↦ s(e)`. The paper assumes every leakage model includes these. — [ct-verif paper, Example 1](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity16/sec16_paper_almeida.pdf)
- Address-based model: an assignment exposes the indices read and written, `⟨s, x0[e0] := e⟩ ↦ s(e0) s(e1) ··· s(en)`; "the base variables need not be leaked as they can be inferred from the control flow". — [ct-verif paper, Example 2](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity16/sec16_paper_almeida.pdf)
- Operand-based model: `⟨s, x[e1] := e2 / e3⟩ ↦ S(e2, e3)` "where S is some function over the operands of the division operation, e.g., the maximum size of the two operands". — [ct-verif paper, Example 3](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity16/sec16_paper_almeida.pdf)
- Definition 1: with `Xi` the public inputs and `Xo` the publicly observable outputs, "A program is secure when all of its initially Xi-equivalent and finally Xo-equivalent executions are indistinguishable." The authors describe this as a restriction of delimited release in which escape hatches (public output annotations) may occur only in the final state. — [ct-verif paper, Definition 1](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity16/sec16_paper_almeida.pdf)
- The introduction ties the path-only policy to "security in the program counter model", the address policy to cache-timing attacks, and operand policies to instructions such as "integer division and floating point operations". — [ct-verif paper, §1](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity16/sec16_paper_almeida.pdf)

Barthe, Betarte, Campo, Luna, Pichardie, "System-level non-interference for constant-time cryptography" (CCS 2014)
- The abstract states the paper proposes "a new information-flow analysis that checks if an x86 application executes in constant-time, or in S-constant-time" (a weaker notion for programs using stealth memory), proves such programs do not leak through the cache to concurrently running operating systems, and formalizes the results in Coq. Case studies: PolarSSL AES, DES, RC4, SHA256, Salsa20. — [ePrint 2014/422](https://ia.cr/2014/422)
- Later papers cite it for the result that justifies the model: Vale says "As shown by Barthe et al., a program is free of cache-based side channels if it does not branch on secrets, and if it performs no secret-dependent memory accesses". — [Vale paper, §3.1](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity17/sec17-bond.pdf)
- The Jasmin group states the same result with its hypotheses: the two rules protect against "a powerful low-level adversary with control over the cache and the scheduler, provided the victim program and the adversary execute in different processes, and memory isolation between processes are guaranteed". — [Structured leakage, CCS 2021, §2.3](https://members.loria.fr/VLaporte/files/CCS2021_StructuredLeakage.pdf)

Catalogue of leakage models (Barthe, Grégoire, Laporte, CSF 2018)
- Observational non-interference (Definition 1): `P ⊨ ONI(φ)` iff for initial states `a, a'` with `φ a a'`, `a →t n b ∧ a' →t' n b'` implies `t = t' ∧ (b final ⇔ b' final)`. Definition 4: cryptographic constant-time w.r.t. φ is ONI in the constant-time leakage model. — [CSF 2018 paper, §III–IV](https://members.loria.fr/VLaporte/files/CSF2018_ConstantTimePreservation.pdf)
- Listed policies and the type of the leakage: step-counting (list of unit); program counter (list of booleans, all guards evaluated); memory obliviousness (list of accessed addresses, not values); constant-time (heterogeneous list of booleans and addresses); cost obliviousness (list of numbers); size-respecting (size of operands for specific operators such as division, from a finite set); constant-time with size (booleans, addresses and sizes). — [CSF 2018 paper, §IV.D and Fig. 3](https://members.loria.fr/VLaporte/files/CSF2018_ConstantTimePreservation.pdf)
- Instrumented semantics (Fig. 2): `leak(a[e], ρ) = leak(e, ρ)·λa(a, [e]ρ)`; a conditional steps with leakage `leak(e, ρ)·λif([e]ρ)`; an array store with `leak(e1, ρ)·leak(e2, ρ)·λa(a, [e1]ρ)`. In the constant-time model `λif(z) = [z ≠ 0]` and `λa(a, z) = [(a, z)]`. — [CSF 2018 paper, Fig. 2–3](https://members.loria.fr/VLaporte/files/CSF2018_ConstantTimePreservation.pdf)
- Non-cancellation: compiler-preservation proofs need leakage where equality of whole-run leakage implies stepwise equality; program-counter and constant-time models are made non-cancelling by emitting a marker `[•]` instead of the empty leakage. — [CSF 2018 paper, §IV.E](https://members.loria.fr/VLaporte/files/CSF2018_ConstantTimePreservation.pdf)

Survey view (SoK: Computer-Aided Cryptography, S&P 2021)
- "The simplest leakage model is the program counter policy, where the program control-flow is leaked"; the constant-time policy "additionally assumes that memory accesses are leaked"; the size-respecting policy "also assumes that operand sizes are leaked for specific variable-time operations". — [SoK CAC, §IV.A](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/cac-sok.pdf)
- Variable-time operation leakage is described as including "inputs to variable-time operations (e.g., floating point operations, division and modulus operations on some architectures) classified according to timing-equivalent ranges". — [SoK CAC, §IV.B](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/cac-sok.pdf)
- "Implementation-level proofs are only as good as their models"; the constant-time policy captures "logical timing side channels in a simple model of hardware" and does not cover speculative or out-of-order execution. — [SoK CAC, §IV](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/cac-sok.pdf)

Variable-time instruction models in tools
- Vale: the machine semantics records in the trace "all inputs to variable-latency instructions", in addition to branches and memory-access locations. — [Vale paper, §3.1](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity17/sec17-bond.pdf)
- Bedrock2 (Conoly, Erbsen, Chlipala; PLDI 2025): "division and modulo leak their arguments". — [arXiv 2504.15550, §8.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15550)
- Jasmin `jasmin-ct --doit`: arguments to any instruction not on the vendor data-operand-independent-timing list must be public, checked after instruction selection. The docs name Intel DOIT, Arm DIT and RISC-V Zkt as the target references, and give `LZCNT` as an instruction accepted in normal mode and rejected in DOIT mode. — [Jasmin constant-time docs](https://jasmin-lang.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tools/ct.html)
- The Jasmin group notes the baseline model is "sometimes too coarse, sometimes too precise" (time-variable `div`; cache-line granularity) and that "Leakage models form a lattice" (citing Shivakumar, Barthe, Grégoire, Laporte, Priya 2022). — [Laporte, GLSec slides, 2022-11-24](https://members.loria.fr/VLaporte/files/GLSEC22_FineGrainedConstantTimePolicies.pdf)
- `crypto-bigint` (RustCrypto) states: "this library is NOT suitable for use on processors with a variable-time multiplication operation". — [crypto-bigint 0.7.11 docs](https://docs.rs/crypto-bigint/latest/crypto_bigint/)

### Inferences
- "Trace" has two shapes in the literature. Flat: a list of events appended per step (ct-verif, CSF 2018, Low*, Vale, CompCert-CT, EasyCrypt extraction). Structured: a tree mirroring the derivation (Jasmin, CCS 2021), introduced only because compiler passes need to transform leakage. For a source-level proof with no compiler-preservation theorem, the flat list is sufficient.
- A source-level model over a language without addresses can still express the memory-access policy by leaking array indices, as ct-verif's while-language and Jasmin's source semantics do; the base object is determined by control flow.
- Shifts and multiplication are not variable-time in any formal model read here; they are handled generically by "not on the DOIT list implies operands must be public". Whether a given shift or multiply is on the list is a per-CPU fact outside the formal development.

### Gaps
- The CCS 2014 paper's own formal definitions (its type system, its notion of trace) could not be read: ePrint, HAL, IRISA and CORE copies all refused automated access. Only its abstract and other papers' characterisations of it are reported.
- No source read gives a formal operand-leak rule specifically for shifts or multiplication.
- Shivakumar et al. "Enforcing fine-grained constant-time policies" (CCS 2022) was not read directly; it is known here only through the Laporte slides.

## Key Question 2: How does each tool chain concretely establish constant-time?

### Takeaway
There are four proof styles: (a) a security type system or taint analysis that keeps secrets away from every leaking position (Jasmin `jasmin-ct`, Low*/HACL* abstract secret integers, Vale's verified analyzer, FaCT, Rust secret-integer crates); (b) relational or product-program deductive proof that two runs leak the same trace (ct-verif, Jasmin via EasyCrypt pRHL, HOL Light machine-code RHL); (c) a unary proof that the trace is a function of public inputs (Bedrock2); (d) proofs that a compiler preserves the property (CompCert-CT, Jasmin). Everything else (Binsec/Rel, ctgrind, dudect, ct-fuzz, MemSan, Revizor) checks or tests the binary without a foundational proof.

ct-verif
- Construction: `product(p)` assumes `x = x̂` for public inputs, then at each subprogram asserts `L(p) = L(p̂)` and runs `p; p̂` on renamed copies, preserving the original control structure; "product program Q has only as many paths as P itself". — [ct-verif paper, §4, Fig. 7–8](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity16/sec16_paper_almeida.pdf)
- Theorem 1: "A safe program with (respectively, without) public outputs is secure if (respectively, iff) its output-insensitive product is safe." Theorem 2 gives an iff for the output-sensitive product "with some benign-leakage annotation". — [ct-verif paper, §4](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity16/sec16_paper_almeida.pdf)
- Public outputs: the output-sensitive product records observations in history variables along branches marked benign, assumes equality of public outputs at the end, then asserts the histories are equal. — [ct-verif paper, Fig. 9](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity16/sec16_paper_almeida.pdf)
- Loop invariants "are simply conjunctions of equalities between some program variables and their shadowed copies". — [ct-verif paper, §5.4](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity16/sec16_paper_almeida.pdf)
- Level and trust: verification is done on optimized LLVM assembly. The authors argue path- and address-based properties "are generally preserved" by code generation but "Operand-based constant-time properties, however, are generally not preserved" because instruction selection can change operand types. — [ct-verif paper, §5.5](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity16/sec16_paper_almeida.pdf)
- The reduction for the while language (output-insensitive case) is formalized in Coq. — [ct-verif paper, §4](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity16/sec16_paper_almeida.pdf)

Jasmin
- Two methods: "largely automatic but incomplete" type system (`jasmin-ct`), and EasyCrypt extraction with explicit leakage, which is more expressive and can prove probabilistic constant-time. Both assume the program is already proved safe. — [Jasmin constant-time docs](https://jasmin-lang.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tools/ct.html)
- What must be public: `if`/`while` conditions, load and store addresses, array indices, `for` bounds. A conditional expression `c ? e1 : e2` does not require `c` public because it compiles to a conditional move. A memory load is always typed `secret`. — [Jasmin constant-time docs](https://jasmin-lang.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tools/ct.html)
- Type system rules (two-point lattice L ≤ H, flow-sensitive environment, "public branching only (no implicit flows)"): Cond requires `Γ ⊢ b : L`; Store requires the pointer `L`; Assign-Array requires the index `L` and sets `a ← τ ⊔ Γ(a)`. — [Laporte, GLSec slides 2022](https://members.loria.fr/VLaporte/files/GLSEC22_FineGrainedConstantTimePolicies.pdf)
- Declassification: `() = #declassify(value);` relabels a local as public; the checker does not verify that the value is public, and each use "must be justified by other means". — [Jasmin constant-time docs](https://jasmin-lang.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tools/ct.html)
- EasyCrypt route: `jasmin2ec -m CT` produces a module whose procedures accumulate a leakage value; the theorem is a pRHL equivalence whose postcondition equates the leakages, proved by `proc; inline *; sim`. In the slides the statement is `[c] ∼ [c] : φ ∧ ={leak} ⟹ ={leak}` with extracted code of the form `leak <- LeakAddr([0]) :: leak; aux <- a.[0]; … leak <- LeakCond(x \ult y) :: LeakAddr([]) :: leak; if (x \ult y) {…}`. — [Jasmin constant-time docs](https://jasmin-lang.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tools/ct.html); [Laporte, GLSec slides 2022](https://members.loria.fr/VLaporte/files/GLSEC22_FineGrainedConstantTimePolicies.pdf)
- Compiler preservation: Definition 6.1, "A program p is cryptographic constant-time w.r.t. the indistinguishability relation ∼ when … ∀ s1 s2, s1 ∼ s2 ⟹ ∃ s1' s2' ℓ, p : s1 ⇓ℓ s1' ∧ p : s2 ⇓ℓ s2'" (this strengthened form also implies safety). Theorem 6.3: if the source is CCT w.r.t. ∼ and compilation succeeds, the target is CCT w.r.t. the lifted relation, which additionally requires equal stack pointers and enough free stack. Proved in Coq as a corollary of "instrumented correctness": each pass outputs a leakage transformer `F` with target leakage equal to `F` applied to source leakage. — [Structured leakage, CCS 2021, §2.3, §6.1](https://members.loria.fr/VLaporte/files/CCS2021_StructuredLeakage.pdf)
- Programs that are leakage-free without being constant-time (for example, rejection sampling) are treated by Almeida, Firsov, Oliveira, Unruh with a pRHL characterisation in EasyCrypt. — [ePrint 2023/1514](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1514)
- The newest Jasmin semantics (interaction trees in Rocq, arXiv v2 dated 16 Aug 2026) uses event families Call, Err, Rnd and excludes side-channel leakage from scope: the adversary "cannot observe their side-channel leakage". Its stated trusted base includes Jasmin's preprocessor and printer, the x86-64 formalization, and the Rocq kernel and OCaml compiler. — [arXiv 2511.11292](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11292)

Low* / HACL* / F*
- Method: "we use type abstraction to ensure that our code's branching and memory access patterns are secret independent." Secret limbs have an abstract type with no comparison returning `bool`; `eq_mask` is provided instead, and `v : limb → Ghost nat` reveals values only in ghost code. — [Low* paper, §2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.00053)
- Trace grammar: `ℓ ::= · | read(b, n, f) | write(b, n, f) | brT | brF | ℓ1, ℓ2`. The model is "the program counter model [Molnar et al. 2006] augmented to track potential leaks through cache behavior [Barthe et al. 2014]". — [Low* paper, §3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.00053)
- Theorem 3.1 (Secret independence): given a program well-typed against a secret interface Γs, a well-typed implementation Ps of that interface that is "equivalent modulo secrets", and two well-typed substitutions ρ1, ρ2, either both instances are stuck or "both programs make progress with the same trace" to related configurations. Obligation on the trusted library: each `f ∈ Ps`, applied in related stores to related values, returns related results "while producing identical traces". — [Low* paper, §3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.00053)
- Level: enforced at source, with "our compilation carries such properties to the level of Clight"; below that, "one may validate the output of the C compiler by relying on recent tools proving side-channel resistance at the assembly level". — [Low* paper, §2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.00053)
- Trusted: F* typechecker, Z3, "the manual proofs of the metatheory relating the semantics of λow* to CompCert Clight", KreMLin ("implemented in unverified OCaml"), and the C compiler. — [Low* paper, §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.00053)
- In HACL*'s `Lib.IntTypes`, `type secrecy_level = | SEC | PUB`; `div`, `mod`, `eq`, `ne`, `lt`, `lte`, `gt`, `gte` take `PUB` arguments, while `eq_mask`, `neq_mask`, `gte_mask`, `lt_mask`, `gt_mask`, `lte_mask` take and return `SEC`. There is a `secret` function from PUB to SEC and no function from SEC to PUB. — [Lib.IntTypes.fsti](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hacl-star/hacl-star/main/lib/Lib.IntTypes.fsti)
- The escape hatch is a separate module whose header says "using the u*_to_UInt* functions BREAKS secret independence." — [Lib.RawIntTypes.fsti](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hacl-star/hacl-star/main/lib/Lib.RawIntTypes.fsti)

Vale
- Specification (Fig. 9): `isLeakageFree(code, pubStartLocs, pubEndLocs)` holds when for all `s, t, s', t'` with `evalCode(code, s, s')`, `evalCode(code, t, t')`, agreement on `pubStartLocs` and `s.trace == t.trace`, we get `s'.trace == t'.trace` and agreement on `pubEndLocs`. `trace` is a ghost field of the machine state. — [Vale paper, §3.1](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity17/sec17-bond.pdf)
- Attacker: sees "each instruction executed, all memory locations each instruction accesses, and any other instruction inputs that can influence timing". — [Vale paper, §3.1](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity17/sec17-bond.pdf)
- Proof: a one-time, machine-checked (Dafny) soundness proof of an analyzer, `LeakageFree ⟹ isLeakageFree(Code, PubStartLocs, PubEndLocs)`; the analyzer is "a straightforward flow-sensitive dataflow analysis" over a Public/Secret lattice that returns false if a branch predicate or a memory base/offset may be non-public. Memory taint reuses aliasing facts from the functional proof. Only successful runs are considered, justified by the functional verification. — [Vale paper, §3.2–3.3](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity17/sec17-bond.pdf)

CompCert-CT (Barthe, Blazy, Grégoire, Hutin, Laporte, Pichardie, Trieu; POPL 2020)
- CompCert traces are enriched "with two kinds of leakages: the truth value of a condition, a pointer representing the address of either a memory access or a called function". A program is constant-time secure w.r.t. φ if leak-only executions from φ-related initial states observe the same leakage. — [Blazy slides, 2019-09-25](https://wp.doc.ic.ac.uk/vetss/wp-content/uploads/sites/122/2019/10/Blazy-Formal-Verification-of-a-Constant-Time.pdf)
- Method: modify CompCert to remove leaky constructs, instrument the semantics of each intermediate language, and prove preservation with proof techniques that reuse the existing simulation proofs. — [ePrint 2019/926](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/926)
- The underlying framework defines lockstep, manysteps and general "CT-simulations" (pairs of relations on two source runs and two target runs) and proves that each yields preservation of the constant-time policy. — [CSF 2018 paper, Definitions 5–12, Theorems 1, 4, 6](https://members.loria.fr/VLaporte/files/CSF2018_ConstantTimePreservation.pdf)
- SoK remark: "CompCert-generated assembly code is not as efficient as that generated by GCC and Clang". — [SoK CAC, §IV.C](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/cac-sok.pdf)

Bedrock2 / Fiat Cryptography (Conoly, Erbsen, Chlipala; PLDI 2025)
- Constant-time is stated in single-copy form: "∃f. ∀ public/private inputs. p ⇓ {(t,k) | k = f(public)}", where `k` is the leakage trace of memory addresses and branch outcomes. With compiler-resolved nondeterminism (stack addresses), the trace is a function of an oracle or, equivalently, is produced by a "predictor" mapping a trace prefix to the next event. — [arXiv 2504.15550](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15550)
- Trusted base: "The only other trusted code here (other than Coq) is our leakage-augmented RISC-V semantics". — [arXiv 2504.15550, §8](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15550)
- On Fiat Cryptography: it "produces (with a Coq-verified compiler) performance-critical inner loops that are straightline code without explicit memory access" and so is "trivially verified as constant time". — [arXiv 2504.15550, related work](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15550)
- Modularity caveat: predictor-based specifications are "unfortunately nonmodular"; Lemma D.1 concatenates a predictor for `f1`'s leakage with a function from that leakage to a predictor for what follows. — [arXiv 2504.15550, Appendix D](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15550)

hax / libcrux (Cryspen)
- F* backend (2024-01-30): "In F*, we treat `i32` as a secret integer type"; "a public integer must be explicitly labeled as `pub_i32`"; "operations like `%` are not defined for secret integers", so `input % 3329` is rejected by the typechecker. The analysis found a timing bug in their ML-KEM code. — [Cryspen, Verifying Libcrux's ML-KEM](https://cryspen.com/post/ml-kem-verification/)
- Rust-side discipline (`libcrux-secrets` 0.0.8, 2026-11-19): secret wrapper types (`U8`, `[I16; N]`, …) with `.classify()`/`.declassify()`; the `check-secret-independence` feature flags branches on comparisons over secrets, array accesses at secret indices, and division or modulus. With the `valgrind_ct_test` cfg, classify marks memory Undefined and declassify marks it Defined so Memcheck flags secret-dependent addresses and control flow. `Select` and `Swap` are described as "best effort"; every `.declassify()` "represents a potential side-channel leak". — [libcrux-secrets docs](https://docs.rs/libcrux-secrets/latest/libcrux_secrets/)
- Scope (2026-02-12): Cryspen "do not prove formal guarantees of side-channel resistance for the compiled executable"; constant-time code can "be turned into branching code by clever compilers"; verifying side-channel resistance of compiled libcrux is listed as future work. — [Cryspen, The strengths and limits of formal verification](https://cryspen.com/post/strengths-and-limitations/)
- Lean: hax 0.4 (2026-09-29) replaced its Lean backend with one that drives Charon and Aeneas; the old engine backend is `legacy-lean`. None of the 2026 hax blog posts mention secret integers, secret independence or constant-time for Lean. — [hax blog archive 2026](https://hax.cryspen.com/blog/archive/2026/)

FaCT, CryptOpt
- FaCT (2019) is classified as a DSL-level, formal, sound tool; the SoK lists it as a synthesis tool that "can automatically generate secure implementations from insecure implementations". — [CT-tools list](https://crocs-muni.github.io/ct-tools/); [SoK CAC, §IV.B](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/cac-sok.pdf)

Binary-level and testing tools
- Binsec/Rel (Daniel, Bardin, Rezk; S&P 2020): relational symbolic execution on x86 and ARM binaries, no source needed; motivated by constant-time being a two-execution property that compilers do not preserve. 296 implementations analysed. It found violations introduced by gcc -O0 and by clang backend passes in code that an LLVM-level tool had accepted. — [arXiv 1912.08788](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.08788); [Binsec summary](https://binsec.github.io/nutshells/sp-20.html); [extended version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.01129)
- Classification from the CT-tools list: ct-verif (2016, LLVM IR, formal, sound); ctgrind (2010, binary, dynamic, sound with restrictions); dudect (2017, binary, statistical, no guarantee); ct-fuzz (2020, LLVM IR, dynamic, no guarantee); Binsec (2020, binary, symbolic, sound with restrictions); MemSan (LLVM IR, dynamic, sound with restrictions); TIMECOP (binary, dynamic). The list has 55 tools, none Lean-based, and warns its guarantee column is best effort. — [CT-tools list](https://crocs-muni.github.io/ct-tools/)
- The SoK characterises ctgrind differently: Valgrind with taint annotations checking "constant-address security via dynamic analysis", supporting public inputs but not public outputs, "neither sound nor complete". — [SoK CAC, §IV.B](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/cac-sok.pdf); contradicts the "sound with restrictions" label in the [CT-tools list](https://crocs-muni.github.io/ct-tools/)
- Machine-code deductive proof: a 2025 paper gives a relational Hoare logic for machine code in HOL Light and uses it on s2n-bignum to prove both the constant-time discipline and equivalence of optimized and reference routines. — [arXiv 2505.14348](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14348)
- Developer survey (Jancar et al., S&P 2022): 44 developers of 27 libraries; developers know about timing attacks but often deprioritise them, and the study reports shortcomings in existing analysis tools. — [ePrint 2021/1650](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/1650)
- SoK: "analysis should be performed as close as possible to the executed machine code", since mainstream compilers "are known to optimize away defensive code and even introduce new side-channels". — [SoK CAC, §IV.C](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/cac-sok.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a Rust program already modelled in Lean, only styles (a), (b) and (c) are available without building a verified compiler; (d) is not available for rustc/LLVM at all.
- Type-abstraction (Low* style) proves the property once, as a metatheorem about the language, and then each program pays nothing. A proof-assistant embedding of generated code has no such metatheorem unless one is proved about the embedding, so per-function relational or unary proofs are the realistic route in Lean.
- Every deductive approach requires safety (no failing run) first or builds it into the definition (ct-verif Theorem 1, Jasmin Definition 6.1, Vale's "successful runs"). An existing functional-correctness proof supplies exactly that.
- Declassification support matters for signature schemes: ct-verif's public outputs and Vale's `pubEndLocs` are the two formulations that let control flow depend on values that are secret-derived but published. This will be needed if any branch or index depends on data that only becomes public through the output.

- FaCT's transformation and proof details were not read from the primary paper; only the two secondary classifications above are reported.
- Nothing was found stating that CryptOpt proves constant-time; its verified artifact is a functional-equivalence checker. Whether the project claims more could not be confirmed.
- CompCert-CT's treatment of declassification and its exact final theorem statement were not read (ePrint PDF inaccessible; slides and abstract only).
- dudect, ctgrind, ct-fuzz and MemSan primary sources were not read; only the classification table and the SoK remark are reported.
- Two 2026 ePrint papers critical of hax (2026/192 and 2026/670) surfaced in search; they were not read and their relevance to secret independence is unknown. — [ePrint 2026/192](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/192); [ePrint 2026/670](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/670)

## Key Question 3: Is there published or open-source work proving constant-time or noninterference in Lean 4?

### Takeaway
No published Lean 4 proof of constant-time for a real cryptographic implementation was found. What exists is infrastructure: VCVio contains leakage judgments (trace noninterference as a relational triple) for its oracle-computation monad with a one-time-pad example, and two Lean 4 libraries provide probabilistic relational Hoare logics. The flagship Lean 4 crypto verification effort (SymCrypt with Aeneas) explicitly excludes leakage.

### Cited Findings
- VCVio has a module "Leakage Judgments for Side-Channel Reasoning" (copyright 2026, Quang Dao) defining `TraceNoninterference oa₁ oa₂ := RelTriple oa₁ oa₂ (fun z₁ z₂ => z₁.2 = z₂.2)` on computations returning `(result × trace)`, described as "the VCVio analogue of constant-time execution", plus `ProbLeakFree` (equal trace distributions) and `LeakageBound ε` (statistical distance), with lemmas `traceNoninterference_implies_probLeakFree` and `leakageBound_triangle`. — [VCVio, ProgramLogic/Relational/Leakage.lean](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/VCVio/blob/main/VCVio/ProgramLogic/Relational/Leakage.lean)
- Its worked example proves the one-time pad "leak-free under a constant-time observation model", instrumenting automatically with `QueryImpl.withCost` so that each oracle query emits a trace event. The observations are oracle queries, not branches or memory accesses of compiled code. — [VCVio, Examples/OneTimePad/LeakageFree.lean](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/VCVio/blob/main/Examples/OneTimePad/LeakageFree.lean)
- VCVio's paper (ePrint 2026/899, May 2026) describes a "modular relational program logic" extending the Loom framework (POPL 2026) to relational probabilistic reasoning. The abstract does not mention side channels. — [ePrint 2026/899](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/899)
- VCVio also contains an interop file mapping Aeneas's interaction trees to its own (`Aeneas.Data.Coinductive.ITree.toPolyFun`, preserving `pure` and `bind` exactly). — [VCVio, Interop/Aeneas/ITree.lean](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/VCVio/blob/main/Interop/Aeneas/ITree.lean)
- CatCrypt Core (Spitters) is a Lean 4 library with a probabilistic relational Hoare logic (pRHL judgment; sync, frame, reorder rules), described as under active development. — [Lean Reservoir, catcryptCore](https://reservoir.lean-lang.org/@spitters/catcryptCore)
- Lean's built-in `mvcgen` documentation describes a monadic verification-condition generator for a single `do` program and contains no mention of relational logic, noninterference or leakage (docs for Lean 4.37.0-rc1; the page says `mvcgen` is superseded by `vcgen`). — [Lean reference, mvcgen](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/The--mvcgen--tactic/)
- A 2026 arXiv paper proves cardinality bounds on leakage of masked Barrett reduction in Lean 4 with Mathlib. This is hardware masking analysis, not a constant-time proof of software. — [arXiv 2604.24670](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24670)
- The Aeneas/SymCrypt paper (Sept 2026): "we verify only functional correctness, not leakage resistance". — [arXiv 2609.15648, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15648)
- A list of 55 constant-time tools contains no Lean-based tool. — [CT-tools list](https://crocs-muni.github.io/ct-tools/)

### Inferences
- A Lean 4 constant-time proof for Aeneas-extracted code would be new work, not an application of an existing library. The nearest reusable pieces are VCVio's `RelTriple` (already in this project's `.tools/`) and its Aeneas interop, but VCVio's leakage judgments are stated for `OracleComp`, not for Aeneas's `Result`.
- For a deterministic program the relational statement collapses to equality of two pure functions of the inputs, so a full probabilistic relational logic is more machinery than the property requires.

### Gaps
- Lean Zulip archives were not searchable from here; an unpublished discussion or prototype there cannot be ruled out.
- An arXiv paper (2607.17445, on robust downgrading and non-interference) is reported by a secondary review to claim Lean mechanization without an artifact; this was not verified. — [secondary summary](https://pith.science/paper/2607.17445)
- Loom's own paper and repository were not read; that it is unary is inferred from VCVio's statement that it "extends" Loom to the relational setting.

## Key Question 4: Does Aeneas or Charon support leakage, secret types or constant-time? What does SymCrypt claim? What does the ITree-based `Result` model?

### Takeaway
No. Aeneas and Charon have no leakage model, secret types or constant-time support, and Microsoft's SymCrypt proofs state in terms that side channels are out of scope at the Rust level and are handled by fuzzing and symbolic execution of binaries. The interaction-tree `Result` (merged September 2026) currently has exactly one effect, failure, plus a divergence bottom; the stated direction is separation logic for unsafe and concurrent code, and leakage is not mentioned anywhere.

### Cited Findings

SymCrypt
- Trust statement: "although the Rust code is defensively written in constant-time style to protect against microarchitectural side channels, we verify only functional correctness, not leakage resistance." — [arXiv 2609.15648, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15648)
- "Side channels are out of scope at the Rust level: 'constant-time' code is verified for correctness but not leakage protection. Instead, SymCrypt uses leakage-specific fuzzing and symbolic execution on compiled binaries, which provides lower-level informal protection against known microarchitectural side channels on x86-64 platforms." — [arXiv 2609.15648, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15648)
- The same paper trusts "the Rust toolchain, notably its compiler and its platform-specific back-ends", Charon, Aeneas, the Lean kernel, `native_decide` and `bv_decide`, and the axiomatization of intrinsics. — [arXiv 2609.15648, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15648)

### Inferences
- Leakage was not a motivation for the ITree redesign as far as any written record shows: the branch name, README and the only effect implemented all point to separation logic, and a search for leakage terms finds nothing.
- The redesign nevertheless makes a leakage effect expressible in principle (an event with input an observation and output `Unit`), and the generic `EffectWP` could interpret it by appending to a state. But `Result` is fixed to `ITree RustEffect`, the generated code emits no such events, and adding them would need a change to Aeneas's extraction or a separate instrumented copy of the functions. This is a possibility, not an existing feature.
- Because the Aeneas model has no addresses, any Lean-level memory-access policy must be stated over array and slice indices, not machine addresses. This matches ct-verif's source-level formulation but cannot speak about stack spills, table layout or cache lines.
- The `ct_equal_u16` episode shows the limit of functional proofs: the theorem says the mask is correct and nothing about how it is computed.

## Key Question 5: Rust specifics: `subtle`, `secret_integers`, `crypto-bigint`, `zeroize`, `black_box`, and the compiler problem

### Takeaway
Rust has no language-level constant-time guarantee, and the official documentation says so. Every Rust constant-time crate describes itself as best effort, the only stronger claims are for inline-assembly CMOV/CSEL on specific targets, and the libcrux and SymCrypt teams both state that they do not prove side-channel resistance of the compiled code. Binary-level checking in CI is the practice the more careful projects have adopted.

### Cited Findings
- `core::hint::black_box` (docs for Rust 1.100.0, 2026-11-09): it "does not offer any guarantees for cryptographic or security purposes"; "there is no mechanism in the entire Rust language that can provide the guarantees required for constant-time cryptography"; "(There is also no such mechanism in LLVM, so the same is true for every other LLVM-based compiler.)". — [Rust std docs, black_box](https://doc.rust-lang.org/std/hint/fn.black_box.html)
- `subtle` 2.6.1: "This crate represents a 'best-effort' attempt, since side-channels are ultimately a property of a deployed cryptographic system including the hardware it runs on". It hides a `Choice`'s inner `u8` "by passing it through a volatile read". Its `debug_assert`s branch on secrets, so it is meant for release builds. — [subtle docs](https://docs.rs/subtle/latest/subtle/)
- `subtle` has an opt-in `core_hint_black_box` feature for Rust 1.66+. Trail of Bits (2022) describes the failure it guards against as the optimizer "seeing through the black box" and realising a value is boolean, and notes a Rust RFC for secret types was postponed awaiting LLVM support. — [subtle repository](https://github.com/dalek-cryptography/subtle); [Trail of Bits, 2022-01-26](https://blog.trailofbits.com/2022/01/26/part-1-the-life-of-an-optimization-barrier/)
- `cmov` 0.5.4 (RustCrypto): "Conditional move CPU intrinsics which are guaranteed on major platforms to execute in constant-time", implemented with inline `asm!` (x86/x86_64 `CMOVZ`/`CMOVNZ`, aarch64 `CSEL`) so they "will not be rewritten by e.g. LLVM's architecture-specific lowerings" such as x86-cmov-conversion. On other targets a "best effort" fallback uses bitwise arithmetic and `black_box`, and "we cannot guarantee that this implementation generates branch-free" code. The crate has "never been independently audited" and lists RUSTSEC-2026-0003, "Non-constant-time code generation on ARM32 targets". — [cmov README](https://docs.rs/crate/cmov/latest/source/README.md)
- `crypto-bigint` 0.7.11: "Constant-time by default. Variable-time functions are explicitly marked as such" (`*_vartime` suffix). Supported architectures are checked in CI with `cargo checkct` / BINSEC "to ensure that, for functions with `checkct` assertions, changes do not introduce variable-time operations"; other architectures get a portable fallback "without constant-time codegen guarantees". It depends on `ctutils`; its `Choice` provides "a 'best effort' optimization barrier". — [crypto-bigint docs](https://docs.rs/crypto-bigint/latest/crypto_bigint/)
- `secret_integers` 0.1.7 (owners Kiefer and Merigoux; release shown 2026-10-11): wrappers giving "an extra automated check against use of variable-time operations"; "division and direct comparison of these 'secret' integers is disallowed" at compile time, and secret values cannot index memory. "Your crypto code should not contain any `declassify` occurence though to guarantee constant-timedness." — [secret_integers docs](https://docs.rs/secret_integers/latest/secret_integers/)
- `zeroize` uses `core::ptr::write_volatile` and `core::sync::atomic` memory fences so that zeroing is not optimized away. This concerns secret erasure, not timing. — [zeroize source docs](https://docs.rs/zeroize/latest/src/zeroize/lib.rs.html)
- SymCrypt's `wipe_slice` is "implemented via raw pointers and external code to resist compiler optimization", marked opaque and axiomatized in Lean as setting all elements to 0. — [arXiv 2609.15648, §5.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15648)
- LLVM work in progress: Trail of Bits (2025-12-02) describes `__builtin_ct_select`, which "will compile to constant-time machine code, regardless of optimization level" (x86-64 `cmov`, AArch64 CSEL, masked arithmetic elsewhere). It cites "Breaking Bad: How Compilers Break Constant-Time Implementations" (19 libraries, five compilers). At publication the patches were under review, and the Rust compiler team was "exploring" exposure through `core::intrinsics`. Clang patch mail for PR #166703 is dated March 2026. — [Trail of Bits, 2025-12-02](https://blog.trailofbits.com/2025/12/02/introducing-constant-time-support-for-llvm-to-protect-cryptographic-code/); [LLVM branch-commits, March 2026](https://lists.llvm.org/pipermail/llvm-branch-commits/2026-March/087720.html)
- libcrux authors: see Key Question 2 ("do not prove formal guarantees of side-channel resistance for the compiled executable"). — [Cryspen, 2026-02-12](https://cryspen.com/post/strengths-and-limitations/)

### Inferences
- A Lean proof over the Aeneas model is a source-level (MIR-level) proof. Charon extracts MIR before LLVM optimizations, so the proof says nothing about the machine code; the gap is the same one ct-verif narrowed by verifying optimized LLVM IR and Jasmin and CompCert-CT closed with verified compilers.
- The defensible claim for a Rust project is therefore two-part: a machine-checked source-level theorem under a named leakage model, plus a binary-level check (Binsec via `cargo-checkct`, Valgrind-based classification as in `libcrux-secrets`, or statistical testing) per target and compiler version. The second part is evidence, not proof.
- Code that is branch-free at source by masking is the class compilers are known to re-introduce branches into; code whose only data-dependent behaviour is on public values has less exposure to this.

### Gaps
- No evidence was found that `__builtin_ct_select` or an equivalent has shipped in a stable Rust release as of November 2026; current status in rustc was not confirmed.
- No formal statement from the RustCrypto project as a whole was found; only per-crate documentation.
- `ctutils` documentation was not read directly.
- The "Breaking Bad" paper was not read; its figures are as reported by Trail of Bits.

## Key Question 6: What do relational proof rules for a leakage-instrumented monadic language look like?

### Takeaway
Concrete presentations exist at three levels: instrumented operational rules that say what each construct emits (Low*, CSF 2018, Jasmin); the reduction of constant-time to one relational Hoare judgment over a program with an explicit leakage variable (EasyCrypt, Vale); and generic relational rules for monadic programs (Maillard et al.). A unary alternative (Bedrock2) states that the trace equals a function of public inputs and avoids two-run reasoning entirely.

### Cited Findings

Instrumented semantics (what each construct emits)
- Low* small-step rules: `P ⊢ (H, if 0 then e1 else e2) →brF (H, e2)`; a buffer read `let x = readbuf (b, n) n1 in e` steps with `ℓ = read(b, n + n1)`; `newbuf n` emits `write(b, 0), …, write(b, n − 1)`. Traces compose by concatenation `ℓ1, ℓ2`. — [Low* paper, Fig. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.00053)
- CSF 2018 while-language: expressions carry `leak(e, ρ)`; `if` emits `leak(e, ρ)·λif([e]ρ)`; array assignment emits `leak(e1, ρ)·leak(e2, ρ)·λa(a, [e1]ρ)`; sequencing propagates the step label. — [CSF 2018 paper, Fig. 2](https://members.loria.fr/VLaporte/files/CSF2018_ConstantTimePreservation.pdf)
- Jasmin structured leakage: `ℓe ::= • | [z] | ∗p | (ℓe, …, ℓe)` and `ℓ ::= ℓe := ℓe | if_b(ℓe, ℓ) | while_t(ℓe, ℓ, ℓ) | while_f(ℓe) | {ℓ; …; ℓ}`. Rules: `a[e]` evaluates with leakage `(ℓe, [z])` where `z` is the index value; a conditional yields `if_b(ℓe, ℓc)` with `b` the guard value and `ℓc` the taken branch's leakage; a true loop iteration yields `while_t(ℓe, ℓc, ℓw)` and exit yields `while_f(ℓe)`; a sequence yields `{ℓi; ℓc}`. — [Structured leakage, CCS 2021, Fig. 5–6](https://members.loria.fr/VLaporte/files/CCS2021_StructuredLeakage.pdf)
- The structure exists so that compiler passes can emit leakage transformers; with flat concatenation "it is hard to identify the leakages belonging to the sub-parts". — [Structured leakage, CCS 2021, §4](https://members.loria.fr/VLaporte/files/CCS2021_StructuredLeakage.pdf)

Constant-time as one relational judgment
- EasyCrypt: security of `c` w.r.t. low-equivalence φ "is reduced to a Relational Hoare Logic statement: `[c] ∼ [c] : φ ∧ ={leak} ⟹ ={leak}`", where `[·]` makes leakage explicit as assignments to a global list; "Two parameters model: leakage of operators, leakage of memory accesses". — [Laporte, GLSec slides 2022](https://members.loria.fr/VLaporte/files/GLSEC22_FineGrainedConstantTimePolicies.pdf)
- Vale's `isLeakageFree` is the same shape with the trace as a ghost state field and public locations at entry and exit. — [Vale paper, Fig. 9](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity17/sec17-bond.pdf)
- ct-verif's product makes the relational rules syntactic: at a conditional or loop, assert the guards agree, then run both copies under one control structure; relational loop invariants are equalities between variables and their shadow copies. — [ct-verif paper, Fig. 7–8, §5.4](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity16/sec16_paper_almeida.pdf)
- The SoK describes deductive tools as proving "that the leakage traces of two executions of the program coincide if the public parts of the inputs match", with techniques "closely related to the techniques used for functional correctness", usually complete but requiring more interaction than type systems. — [SoK CAC, §IV.B](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/cac-sok.pdf)

Generic relational rules for monadic programs (Maillard, Hrițcu, Rivas, Van Muylder; POPL 2020)
- Judgment `⊢ c1 ∼ c2 { w }` with `c1 : M1 A1`, `c2 : M2 A2` in possibly different monads and `w` a relational specification; semantically `θrel(c1, c2) ≤ w`. — [arXiv 1907.05244](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.05244)
- Ret: `⊢ ret a1 ∼ ret a2 { ret^W (a1, a2) }`. Weaken: from `⊢ c1 ∼ c2 { w }` and `w ≤ w'`. Bind: from `⊢ m1 ∼ m2 { wm }` and `∀ a1 a2, ⊢ f1 a1 ∼ f2 a2 { wf (a1, a2) }` infer `⊢ bind m1 f1 ∼ bind m2 f2 { bind^W wm wf }`. Asymmetric bind variants follow from the monad laws when one side is a `ret`. — [arXiv 1907.05244](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.05244)
- Conditionals: a synchronous rule when both sides branch on conditions the specification ties to the same value, and an asynchronous rule with one premise per branch when only one side is a conditional. Effect-specific rules are given for state, exceptions (ThrowL, ThrowR, Catch) and nondeterminism. — [arXiv 1907.05244](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.05244)

Relational logic over interaction trees
- Jasmin's 2026 Rocq development has judgment `IP, IQ ⊧ c1 ∼ c2 : Φ ⇒ Ψ`, with event pre- and postconditions `IP`, `IQ` alongside state conditions, and rules Assign, While (synchronous, Φ as invariant), Rand, Call, one-sided AssignL, Trans and Rec. It is used for compiler correctness; leakage is out of its scope. — [arXiv 2511.11292](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11292)

Unary alternative
- Bedrock2: "∃f. ∀ public/private inputs. p ⇓ {(t,k) | k = f(public)}". Leakage accumulates in the big-step/weakest-precondition state, so ordinary Hoare-style reasoning applies. Composition needs care: to reason about `f2` after `f1` one must also specify that the value `f1` passes on is a function of `f1`'s leakage (Lemma D.1). — [arXiv 2504.15550, §2 and Appendix D](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15550)

Lean 4
- VCVio states trace noninterference as `RelTriple oa₁ oa₂ (fun z₁ z₂ => z₁.2 = z₂.2)` over computations returning `(result × trace)`. — [VCVio, Relational/Leakage.lean](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/VCVio/blob/main/VCVio/ProgramLogic/Relational/Leakage.lean)
- Aeneas translates Rust loops with a fixed-point combinator `def loop (body : α → Result (ControlFlow α β)) (x : α) : Result β`, defined by `partial_fixpoint`, where `ControlFlow` has constructors `cont` (continue) and `done` (break). — [Aeneas, Std/Primitives.lean](https://github.com/AeneasVerif/aeneas/blob/main/backends/lean/Aeneas/Std/Primitives.lean)

### Inferences
The following is a derived design for Aeneas output, not something any source implements. It is one way to combine the cited pieces.

- Instrumented monad. Use a writer over `Result`, for example `LResult α := Result (α × List Event)` with `Event ::= br Bool | idx Nat | op OpKind Operands`. Erasure `Prod.fst <$> f_instr x = f x` should be proved in Lean for each instrumented function so the existing functional-correctness theorems transfer.
- Emission rules, taken from the cited semantics. `pure` emits nothing. Bind concatenates. `if b then t else e` (and `match`) emits `br b` then the taken branch's events (CSF 2018 `λif`; Jasmin `if_b`). Array or slice read or write at index `i` emits `idx i` (ct-verif Example 2; Jasmin `[z]`). A loop emits, per iteration, whether the body returned continue or done (Jasmin `while_t`/`while_f`). Division and remainder emit their operands or a size class (ct-verif Example 3; Bedrock2). A bitwise select emits nothing (Jasmin's treatment of `c ? e1 : e2`), which is only as true as the compiler makes it.
- Statement, relational form. For φ relating inputs that agree on public parts: `φ x x' → (f_instr x).map Prod.snd = (f_instr x').map Prod.snd`. Statement, unary form: `∃ L, ∀ pub sec, (f_instr pub sec).map Prod.snd = ok (L pub)`. For deterministic total functions the two are interderivable; the unary form turns each lemma into an equation that `simp` can chain, at the cost of writing `L` explicitly (or leaving it existential and composing with a Lemma D.1-style lemma).
- Proof rules that fall out. Bind: the continuation's leak must be public-determined for the value actually produced, so each callee lemma needs a second clause stating which parts of its result are functions of public data. This is the Low* "related results, identical traces" obligation and Bedrock2's Appendix D point. Conditional: prove the guard is public-determined, then both runs take the same branch and the rule is synchronous; a secret guard already violates the property because `br b` is emitted. Loop: the invariant is "leak so far is public-determined and the public part of the loop state is public-determined", the direct analogue of ct-verif's shadow-equality invariants. Array access: prove the index is public-determined.
- Failure. `Result.fail` and bounds-check panics are observable control flow. The existing proof that the function returns `ok` on valid inputs discharges this, mirroring the "safe program" precondition of ct-verif, Jasmin and Vale.
- Where instrumentation comes from. Aeneas emits uninstrumented definitions, so the instrumented copy must be produced by a Lean metaprogram over the generated terms, by a patched Aeneas extraction, or by hand. Hand-written copies must be tied back by the erasure lemma or they are unverified models. A future leakage event in `RustEffect` would remove the duplication but does not exist today.
- Declassification. If any guard or index depends on a value derived from secrets but published in the output, the plain statement is false and one of two repairs is needed: ct-verif's public-output formulation (quantify only over run pairs whose outputs agree on the declared public outputs), or an explicit declassify event whose argument is added to the public side, as in Jasmin, with a separate argument that the value is public.
- What stays trusted. The leakage model's adequacy for the target CPU; the faithfulness of instrumentation where not proved by erasure; Charon and Aeneas; rustc/LLVM preserving the property, for which Rust's own documentation says no mechanism exists; and hardware data-independent timing (DIT/DOIT) being enabled.
- A type-system route in Rust source (secret-integer newtypes in the style of `libcrux-secrets` or `secret_integers`) is cheaper and catches the same three violation classes at compile time, but yields no Lean theorem and treats every declassification as an unchecked assertion. It combines well with the Lean route as a lint.

### Gaps
- No source presents relational rules specifically for a shallowly embedded, leakage-instrumented `Result`/writer monad in Lean; the design above is assembled from the cited deep-embedding and generic-monad presentations.
- The iteration (loop) rule and the noninterference example in Maillard et al. were in the part of the paper not read.
- The Seq and conditional rules of the Jasmin ITree logic are in an appendix that was not read.
- The CCS 2021 leakage-transformer semantics (Fig. 7–8) were not extracted in detail, as they matter only for a compiler-preservation proof.
- Low*'s appendix proof of Theorem 3.1 (the logical-relation argument) was located but not read in detail.
