The generation, signing and verification composition returns `ok true` for three arbitrary seed blocks and correctly sized key output buffers, arbitrary messages, valid contexts, signing randomness and key-generation pool outcomes. ### Progress of the uncached SHAKE signer

The public theorems cover Apple Silicon, Linux Arm and Linux x86-64. For any maximal normal history with at least one participating worker and enough counter capacity, Lean derives the completion order and proves that every participant retires. From any finite prefix of the two queues, Lean constructs a maximal continuation that preserves the recorded stores. Each enabled claim, store or retirement decreases a natural-number measure. The argument assumes threads can execute normal transitions. OS starvation, allocation failure and panic unwinding are outside its scope. [Worker transitions and progress](MaximalWorker.lean) · [Public signing and round trips](MaximalPublic.lean)

The first phase has a single physical queue. Its store history determines the order for XMSS chunks, XMSS leaves and direct FORS fields. The last finisher writes the remaining FORS fields during reduction, and a second queue publishes the layer WOTS signatures. Each table uses its own projection even when two table lengths coincide. The source check records each job’s stores in program order and checks their ownership against Lean for all 24 combinations of width, parameter set and SIMD policy. [Queue projections](MaximalSession.lean) · [Production schedule replay](../../scripts/check-signing-schedules.py)

For each host and parameter set, Lean also checks that enabling a pool with at least two configured threads returns the full signature from the worker. The caller may participate even if a helper is unavailable. A dependency check follows all 216 maximal-history and finite-prefix theorem statements into their two queue projections, the native FORS and XMSS computations, root walk and final serialization.
