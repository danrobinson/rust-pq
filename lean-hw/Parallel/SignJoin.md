### Progress of the uncached SHAKE signer

The public theorems cover Apple Silicon, Linux Arm and Linux x86-64. For any maximal normal history with at least one participating worker and enough counter capacity, Lean derives the completion order and proves that every participant retires. From any finite prefix of the two queues, Lean constructs a maximal continuation that preserves the recorded stores. Each enabled claim, store or retirement decreases a natural-number measure. The argument assumes threads can execute normal transitions. OS starvation, allocation failure and panic unwinding are outside its scope. ### Correspondence with production Rust

Run `python3 scripts/check-worker-mir.py` with the repository's pinned toolchains. The gate compiles the unchanged crate and the staged crate separately for Apple Arm, Linux Arm and Linux x86-64. It compares the normal MIR for the caller, ticket dispatch, completion counter and last-finisher branch, reduction, READY publication, spin and park paths, and WOTS phase. The comparison retains call operands, branch cases, bounds checks and destination slots. It resolves all thirty captures and the arguments at the outlined calls. Separate comparisons cover the nested SIMD, sorting, initialization and mutex recovery callbacks. Lean checks equality of the paired normalized graphs and their finite execution traces for a common interpretation of each block. The MIR reader, closure conversion rules and region boundaries are trusted translation code. [MIR normalization](../../scripts/parallel-proof/worker_mir.py) · [Trace theorem](WorkerMir.lean)

The scratch extraction gives each of the three counters and three reducer output cells a named constructor. The five table constructors also take the element index. Without these labels Aeneas's pure-call simplification merges the counters and the same-typed authentication and roots cells in the caller. The gate checks the constructor bodies and destinations in compiled MIR, checks every pool capture and indexed allocation callback, and proves that erasing each label recovers the original library constructor. The production crate uses the original Rust allocations. [Allocation checks](../../scripts/parallel-proof/worker_allocations.py)

The effectful worker uses a typed heap for one signing invocation. A cell address identifies both its table and index. Lean proves that writing one address preserves every other address. The extraction gate checks equations for the generated dispatch, reduction, phase-one loop, WOTS loop and complete worker. Specializing the effect parameters to the generated interfaces recovers their original Lean bodies. A concurrent interpretation suspends at atomic operations and cell accesses and allows another callback to run. The pool proof identifies the callback and borrowed object at dispatch.
